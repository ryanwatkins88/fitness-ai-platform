# Coaching Guide: Programming Resistance Training for GLP-1 Clients

Internal reference for building and running the Muscle Guard 6-week beta — not client-facing marketing. Written for a coach who has general training knowledge but hasn't specifically trained GLP-1 clients yet.

---

## 1. Before You Start: Screening & Scope

You're currently working through a Weight Loss Management Specialist certification (Chapter 2) — that means right now you're a **fitness and accountability coach**, not a medical provider or registered dietitian. Set that boundary up front with every client, not just for liability, but because it's the same honesty standard the rest of this content is built on.

**Intake, before anyone starts week 1:**
- Basic health history and current activity level (true beginner / returning after a break / currently active).
- Which GLP-1 medication, current dose, and roughly how long they've been on it (titration-phase clients need a different starting intensity than someone stable on their dose for months).
- Current GI symptoms (nausea, constipation, reflux) — these directly affect what training and eating around sessions looks like.
- A note that they should mention this program to their prescriber, especially if anything here would change what they're already doing.
- A simple waiver: you're coaching training/accountability, not adjusting their medication or medical care.

**What's out of scope, always:**
- Recommending they start, stop, or change their dose.
- Managing severe or persistent side effects (that's a "call your prescriber" referral, not a coaching problem).
- Interpreting bloodwork or overriding protein/calorie guidance their doctor or dietitian already gave them.

---

## 2. Core Principles Behind the Programming

- **Resistance training is the primary lever for muscle retention** — cardio burns calories but doesn't give the body a strong reason to keep muscle. Every client track should include lifting, not just movement.
- **Protein target:** general benchmark of ~120-140g/day, adjusted for the individual's bodyweight (heavier clients need more), distributed as 20-30g per meal rather than one big serving — appetite suppression makes large single meals hard, so smaller, more frequent, protein-forward meals work better in practice.
- **The deficit is doing the fat-loss work already.** Your programming job isn't to add more calorie burn — it's to give the body a reason to protect muscle while the deficit does its job. That changes how you think about volume and intensity compared to a non-GLP-1 client.

---

## 3. Customizing to the GLP-1 Phase

**Titration phase (first 4-8 weeks on a new dose, or right after a dose increase):** GI side effects (nausea, low appetite, fatigue) are usually at their worst here. Start conservative — lower volume, moderate intensity, focus on technique and consistency over pushing hard. This is not the phase to chase PRs.

**Stabilized phase (established on a steady dose for a while):** Side effects usually ease. This is where you can progressively add load and volume more confidently, since energy and appetite tend to be more predictable.

**Injection-day cycle (weekly injections, most common):** Appetite and energy typically dip most in the 24-72 hours after injection, then recover through the rest of the week.
- **Schedule harder/heavier sessions later in the injection week**, when energy and appetite have recovered.
- **Keep sessions lighter, shorter, or technique-focused in the 1-3 days post-injection**, when GI symptoms and fatigue are more likely.
- If a client injects, say, Sunday night, a common pattern is: light/rest Mon-Tue, moderate Wed, harder sessions Thu-Sat.

---

## 4. The Workout Framework

Use one structure, scaled by experience level — the framework is the same, the load/complexity is what changes.

**Format: Full-body, 3x/week, non-consecutive days** (e.g., Mon/Wed/Fri). This hits each major muscle group with enough frequency to signal "keep this muscle" without requiring the recovery capacity of a 5-6 day split — important given clients are in a calorie deficit with possibly reduced energy.

**Per session, hit each of these five movement patterns:**
1. A squat or leg press pattern (lower body push)
2. A hinge pattern (deadlift variation, hip thrust)
3. A horizontal push (push-up, chest press)
4. A horizontal or vertical pull (row, lat pulldown)
5. A core/stability movement (plank variation, carry)

**Sets/reps/rest by track:**

| Track | Who it's for | Sets x Reps | Rest | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Foundation** | True beginners, long time off training, or early titration phase | 2 sets x 10-15 reps | 60-90 sec | Bodyweight or machine-based, focus on form, leave 3-4 reps "in the tank" every set |
| **Build** | Some training background, stabilized on dose | 3 sets x 8-12 reps | 90 sec | Free weights/machines, progressive overload, leave 1-2 reps in the tank |
| **Established** | Consistent lifters before starting the GLP-1 | 3-4 sets x 6-10 reps | 90-120 sec | Heavier compound lifts, closer to true effort, still autoregulated (see below) |

**Progression rule:** add weight or reps only when a client completes all prescribed sets at the top of the rep range with good form, two sessions in a row. This is slower than a typical "add weight every week" progression — appropriate here because recovery capacity is reduced by the deficit.

---

## 5. Sample 6-Week Progression (matches the beta program length)

| Week | Focus | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Foundation & technique | Lighter loads across the board, regardless of track — this week is about learning the movements and establishing the habit of 3x/week. |
| 2 | Consistency | Same exercises, same loads — the goal is simply completing all 3 sessions. This is often the hardest week for adherence (energy/GI symptoms still adjusting). |
| 3 | First progression | Add load/reps where the progression rule is met. Reassess protein intake — this is a good checkpoint to confirm they're actually hitting their target. |
| 4 | Build | Continue progressing where earned. Check in on injection-day pattern — are hard sessions landing on their better-energy days? |
| 5 | Push (autoregulated) | The best week to intentionally test a top-end set or slightly heavier load, if energy and recovery have been trending well. Skip this if a client has been consistently fatigued or missing sessions. |
| 6 | Consolidate & assess | Not a max-effort week — this is where you retest baseline strength markers, take final measurements/photos, and review protein/workout adherence for program completion. |

## 6. When to Push vs. Pull Back

Don't program on autopilot — check these each session (a 30-second conversation, not a formal test):

**Push when:**
- Sleep was reasonable, energy is normal-for-them.
- They hit their protein target the day before.
- No new or worsening GI symptoms.
- Previous session felt "good," not "survived."

**Pull back (reduce load, cut a set, or swap to lighter/shorter session) when:**
- They mention nausea, poor sleep, or unusually low appetite that day.
- It's within 1-3 days of an injection or dose increase.
- They missed protein significantly the day before.
- They report feeling lightheaded, dizzy, or unusually wiped out — this is also a moment to ask about hydration and food intake that day, not just push through.

**Rule of thumb:** consistency beats intensity for this population. A client who does a slightly-too-easy session 3x/week for 6 weeks will preserve more muscle than one who does 1-2 brutal sessions and skips the rest from burnout or soreness.

---

## 7. Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Programming like a non-deficit client.** Aggressive volume/intensity progression assumes normal recovery capacity — GLP-1 clients often don't have that margin, especially early on.
- **Ignoring meal timing around sessions.** If a client trains in a low-appetite window, don't expect a big pre-workout meal — plan around a shake or small protein-forward snack instead.
- **Treating a missed or lighter session as failure.** Autoregulation is the plan working, not the plan failing — this matters both for the client's confidence and for how you assess "completion" for the refund.
- **Over-relying on the scale to judge progress.** Track strength trends, measurements, and photos alongside weight — muscle retention won't always show up as a bigger scale drop, and shouldn't be expected to.

---

## 8. Red Flags — Refer Out Immediately, Don't Coach Through

- Severe or persistent abdominal pain (possible pancreatitis).
- Right-upper-quadrant pain or yellowing skin/eyes (possible gallbladder issue).
- Chest pain, fainting, or severe dizziness during or after exercise.
- Signs of significant dehydration (from GI side effects) — very dark urine, dizziness, confusion.
- Anything that reads as a medical emergency — stop the session and tell them to contact their prescriber or seek care immediately. This is never a "modify the workout and monitor" situation.

---

## 9. Monitoring & Tracking (ties to program completion)

Track at intake and at week 6, at minimum:
- Body weight and 2-3 key measurements (waist, arm, thigh — whatever is practical).
- A strength baseline: e.g., load/reps on one squat-pattern, one push, one pull movement — reassess the same three at week 6.
- Progress photos (same pose/lighting/time of day both times).
- Weekly: sessions completed (3/3? fewer?) and a quick protein-adherence check-in.

This is also your practical definition of "completed the workouts and nutrition" for the refund criteria — worth finalizing the exact numbers (e.g., "completed at least X of 18 sessions" and "hit protein target Y% of days") before the beta launches, so it's consistent across every client and clearly stated in your program terms, not just implied.
