# Muscle Guard — Symptom-First Style Guide (No Explicit Drug Callouts)

**Standing rule for all new content going forward:** speak to the exact symptoms and beliefs of someone losing weight fast on a GLP-1 medication — without naming the drug class, a specific brand, or saying "GLP-1 users." The existing 100-question bank, its captions, and the belief-shift file were built before this rule and are left as-is; this is the pattern for everything new.

**Why this works better, not just "safer":** the people actually dealing with this recognize themselves in the symptom the moment you name it accurately — you don't need the drug name to make someone feel called out in the right way. It also reads as universal enough to reach anyone losing weight fast for other reasons (post-surgery, aggressive dieting), which only grows your audience, and it avoids the platform friction that comes with naming prescription medications directly in organic content.

## Language swap table

| Instead of saying... | Say... |
|---|---|
| "on a GLP-1" / "on Ozempic" / "GLP-1 users" | "losing weight fast," "losing weight quickly right now," "on a fast track with your weight loss" |
| "Ozempic face" | "that gaunt, hollow, 'aged overnight' look," "the sunken look that comes with fast weight loss" |
| "your medication" / "the drug" | "however you're losing weight right now," "this approach," or just omit it |
| "your dose" / "your injection" | "this stage of your journey," "wherever you're at right now" |
| semaglutide / tirzepatide / Wegovy / Mounjaro / Zepbound | never use — no brand or chemical names |
| "your prescriber" | "your doctor" is fine on its own — it's not naming the medication |
| appetite suppression, eating way less without trying | keep — this is a genuine symptom, distinctive to the experience, and doesn't name anything |

## The structure: symptom → belief → strategy

Every new piece should do three things, in order:
1. **Call out the symptom** specifically enough that someone living it goes "wait, that's me" — appetite gone, weight falling off fast, gaunt face, feeling weak, shape looking "off."
2. **Challenge the belief** attached to that symptom — usually an internal belief ("I have no discipline," "I'm too far gone") or external belief ("this is just an unavoidable side effect," "my doctor would've said something").
3. **Tease the strategy** — protein target + resistance training, without turning it into a full lesson. Enough to position you as the person who actually has an answer, before the soft invite to Muscle Guard.

## New hook + caption examples (starter batch)

**1. Symptom: appetite gone, forgetting to eat**
Hook: "If you've stopped feeling hungry like you used to, here's what nobody's telling you about what that's doing to your body."
Caption: When your appetite drops that fast, it's easy to assume less food just means less fat — but your body doesn't know the difference between "not hungry" and "not enough protein." Without enough protein, some of what you lose ends up being muscle, not fat. You don't need to force big meals — you need a protein target built around how little you're actually eating right now. That's the piece most plans skip.

**2. Symptom: weight falling off faster than ever before**
Hook: "If the weight is coming off faster than it ever has before, that speed is exactly why your muscle needs extra protection right now."
Caption: Fast weight loss puts more pressure on muscle than slow weight loss does — it's not your imagination that this feels different than past diets. The fix isn't slowing down, it's adding two things alongside the speed: enough protein, and a couple of strength sessions a week. That's the part most people never get told to add.

**3. Symptom: face looks gaunt, hollow, or aged**
Hook: "If your face looks more hollow or tired lately, that's not just 'part of losing weight' — it's usually a sign of something specific."
Caption: A sunken, aged look during rapid weight loss is almost always tied to lean tissue loss, not just fat loss — and lean tissue responds to protein and resistance training the same way muscle anywhere else does. It's not an unavoidable side effect of losing weight fast. It's a signal your body needs backup — and that backup is very learnable.

**4. Symptom: feel weaker despite being lighter**
Hook: "If you feel weaker even though you're lighter, your body is telling you something the scale can't."
Caption: Feeling weaker while getting lighter usually means some of what came off was muscle, not just fat — strength is actually a more honest progress marker than weight in that situation. There's a specific reason for it, and a specific fix: enough protein, and training that tells your body to hold onto what it has.

**5. Belief: "I don't have the discipline"**
Hook: "You think you don't have the discipline for this. The truth is most plans people quit were never built for someone eating this little, this fast."
Caption: If you've tried and stopped before, it's rarely a willpower problem — it's usually that the plan assumed more appetite and energy than you actually have right now. A plan built around 3 sessions a week and a protein target that fits how you're eating today is a very different ask than a generic gym routine.

**6. Belief: "I'm too far gone / too out of shape to start"**
Hook: "'I'm too out of shape to start' is the belief costing you the easiest muscle you'll ever protect."
Caption: The less trained you are right now, the faster your body typically responds to resistance training — that's true at any age, any starting point. "Too far gone" is a story, not a fact about your body.

**7. Belief: "if this mattered, my doctor would've said something"**
Hook: "It's not that nobody would tell you this matters. It's that a 15-minute appointment was never built for this conversation."
Caption: Muscle protection during fast weight loss is a real, researched consideration — it just doesn't fit neatly into a quick check-in focused on your numbers and side effects. That's not your doctor withholding anything. It's a gap in the system, and it's exactly the gap this content is here to fill.

**8. Belief: "fast weight loss and looking toned don't go together"**
Hook: "You've been told fast weight loss and looking toned are two different outcomes. They're not — they're two different variables."
Caption: How fast the weight comes off is one thing. Whether what's left is toned or "deflated" is a completely separate thing, decided by protein intake and whether you're doing resistance training. You don't have to choose slow-and-toned over fast-and-shrunk.

**9. Belief: "protocols/programs are just hype"**
Hook: "You're right to be skeptical of 'protocols.' Here's one that's just two things you can verify yourself."
Caption: This isn't a proprietary product or a secret formula — it's a protein target and a simple resistance training structure, the same two things every serious study on preserving muscle during rapid weight loss points to. Skepticism is healthy. It holds up fine here, because there's nothing to take on faith.

**10. Symptom: clothes looser, but shape looks "off"**
Hook: "If your clothes are looser but your shape looks 'off' instead of toned, that's telling on exactly what came off."
Caption: Looser clothes with a flatter, less defined shape usually means muscle came off along with the fat — the scale can't tell you that, but your shape can. It's an extremely common, very fixable pattern once protein and resistance training are added back in.

**11. Symptom: some weeks harder than others (energy/appetite swings)**
Hook: "If some weeks you have almost zero appetite and others it creeps back, your plan needs to move with that — not ignore it."
Caption: That up-and-down pattern is completely normal with fast, medically guided weight loss, and a plan that ignores it usually gets abandoned within a few weeks. The fix is scheduling lighter weeks for your low-appetite stretches and pushing a bit more when energy comes back.

**12. Belief: "I've failed at fitness before"**
Hook: "Every plan you've quit taught you something. It just wasn't 'you're bad at this.'"
Caption: Most quit points trace back to a plan that didn't match your actual energy and appetite at the time — not a personal failure. A plan built specifically around fast weight loss, with a protein target that flexes with your appetite, is a genuinely different ask than what you've tried before.

**13. Belief: "I don't have time"**
Hook: "You think this takes hours. It takes three sessions, built around the life you already have."
Caption: Protecting muscle during fast weight loss doesn't require a daily gym habit — the research points to 2-3 resistance sessions a week as the effective number. The time cost people picture is usually much bigger than the actual plan.

**14. Symptom: certain foods don't sit right anymore**
Hook: "If certain foods just don't sit right anymore, that's not you being difficult — plan around it, don't fight it."
Caption: When your stomach is pickier right now, forcing big meals or "clean eating" rules usually backfires. The better move is smaller, protein-forward foods that actually sit well — shakes, yogurt, eggs — so you hit your protein number without a fight. It's a logistics problem, not a discipline problem.

**15. Belief: "the gaunt look is just unavoidable"**
Hook: "You've been told that hollow, sunken look is just something that happens. It's something that happens without a plan."
Caption: The "aged overnight" look people describe during fast weight loss tracks closely with lean tissue loss — and lean tissue responds to protein and resistance training just like muscle anywhere else in the body. "Unavoidable" isn't accurate. "Unaddressed" is closer to the truth.

**16. Belief: "I should just be grateful the weight's coming off"**
Hook: "Being grateful the weight's coming off and wanting to look toned aren't opposites. You're allowed both."
Caption: Wanting the number to drop and wanting to keep your shape aren't in competition — they're just two parts of the same goal. Nothing wrong with wanting the whole result instead of only half of it.

**17. Symptom: meals feel like an afterthought**
Hook: "If your meals feel more like an afterthought than a plan, that's exactly when protein gets missed without you noticing."
Caption: When food volume drops this much, it's easy to eat "enough" by feel and still land far under your protein target — appetite isn't a reliable guide to it anymore. Knowing your actual number, not guessing, is the first fix, and it's simpler to hit than most people expect.

**18. Belief: "coaches/programs are just trying to sell me something"**
Hook: "You think this is about the sale. This round, you get every dollar back if you actually do the work."
Caption: This beta is structured so you're betting on yourself, not on me — complete the workouts and the nutrition, and you get a full refund, regardless of where the scale lands. Getting real case studies right now matters more to me than the money.

**19. Symptom: energy tanking hard**
Hook: "If your energy has tanked since the weight started dropping fast, that's usually protein and calories talking, not laziness."
Caption: A steep drop in both food and appetite can leave you under-fueled in ways that show up as exhaustion, not just hunger. Fixing your protein intake often fixes both problems — the fatigue and the muscle risk — at the same time.

**20. Belief: "I don't know enough to do this on my own"**
Hook: "You're not supposed to already know your protein number or how to train around weeks where your appetite disappears. That's what a guide is for."
Caption: Figuring out protein targets, training splits, and how to adjust for a week where you can barely eat isn't something you're expected to know already — it's literally the job of a coach who's paying attention to this specific situation. That's the whole reason to have someone in your corner instead of piecing it together alone.
