# Muscle Guard — Group-to-Beta Conversion Funnel

Maps the path you described: you interact helpfully in Facebook groups → add/follow the person → they check out your profile → your content moves them toward the beta. This is about designing what they actually encounter at each step, not just making more content.

## The funnel, step by step

**Step 1 — Group interaction (where trust starts).** You answer their question or comment genuinely in the group. This is doing real work already — don't rush past it into a pitch. The value of this step is that it's *not* a pitch; keep it that way.

**Step 2 — Add/follow.** Right after adding someone, don't message them yet. Let your profile do the next bit of work. If you DM immediately after adding, it reads as "I only added you to sell you something," which undoes the trust from Step 1.

**Step 3 — They check your profile.** This is the moment your recent posts and pinned content matter most. A cold-to-warm lead usually looks at: your most recent few posts, whatever's pinned to the top, and your bio. Design for that specific 30-second scan.

**Step 4 — They engage (like, comment, or react).** This is your actual buying signal. Respond to it — don't let it sit.

**Step 5 — DM conversation → beta invite.** Only after some real signal of interest, not immediately after adding.

---

## What to put at the top of your profile (pinned content)

Most platforms let you pin posts — use it. Structure the first three things a new visitor sees like this:

1. **Pin #1 — Who you are / what you help with.** A symptom-callout piece (from the style guide) paired with a short bio-style caption: what you help people do, and the fact that you're actively completing your Weight Loss Management Specialist certification (Chapter 2, stated honestly — see prior guidance). No pitch yet.
2. **Pin #2 — Proof/authority.** Once you have any client story, data point, or even just a clear explanation of your research-backed approach, this is where it goes. Until you have real case studies, use a research-backed strategy piece instead (protein + resistance training explainer) — it does the same "I know what I'm talking about" job.
3. **Pin #3 — The direct offer.** A clear, honest explanation of the 6-week beta: what it is, the bet-on-yourself refund structure, and exactly what to do next (comment a keyword or send a DM).

**Below the pins, your regular feed** should show a mix — rotate symptom-callout, belief-shift, and strategy-tease posts so a scrolling visitor sees range, not one repeated pitch.

## DM scripts by scenario

**A. Right after adding someone from a group (no ask yet — just staying warm)**
> "Hey [Name]! Saw your comment in [group] about [specific thing they said] — that really resonated, it's exactly the kind of thing I talk about in my own content. Hope it's alright I added you! How's everything going on your end?"

Goal here is a real conversation, not a pitch. If they respond and it turns into genuine back-and-forth, let it — the pitch can come later, or not from you at all (they may ask what you do).

**B. When someone comments or reacts to one of your posts for the first time**
> "Hey [Name], thanks for the comment on that one — curious, is [specific symptom/belief the post named] something you're dealing with too?"

This keeps it a conversation, not a funnel step, while still qualifying whether they're a real fit.

**C. When someone comments a keyword (e.g. "MUSCLE") on a direct-offer post**
> "Hey [Name]! Saw you comment on the muscle-protection post — that's exactly what my 6-week beta program is built for. Quick version: [1-2 sentence description] and it's structured so if you complete the workouts and nutrition, you get every dollar back. Want me to send you the full rundown?"

This is your only "cold ask" — it's earned because they self-identified by commenting the keyword.

**D. Nurture DM for someone who's liked/reacted to several posts over time but never engaged directly**
> "Hey [Name], noticed you've been checking out some of my posts about muscle loss during fast weight loss — figured I'd ask directly: is that something you're navigating too? Happy to share what's worked for people if it's useful, no pressure."

---

## Sequencing rules

- **Never pitch in the first message after adding someone.** Earn at least one real exchange first, or a clear self-identified signal (keyword comment).
- **Match your ask to their signal strength.** A like gets a soft, curious DM (scenario B/D). A keyword comment gets the direct offer (scenario C). Don't over-pitch a weak signal or under-pitch a strong one.
- **Keep the group behavior itself unchanged** — you're still there to genuinely help, not to farm leads. The funnel works *because* the group interaction is real; if it starts feeling transactional there, it stops working everywhere downstream.
